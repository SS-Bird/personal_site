### 1780s Ensemble
This ensemble was made for my grade 11 personal project, but entirely self directed. The outfit would be appropriate for a working class woman from the 1780s. The stays are an adjusted version of the Redthreaded 1780s front lacing stays and are made with a cotton lining, two layers of mystery ‘linen’, plastic boning, leather binding and stiffened with gum tragacanthin. The rest of the ensemble was not made from a commercial patterned and made with linen.

### Other Historical Pieces
My entirely hand stitched 18th century men’s shirt. This is one of my absolute favorite creations. The classic piece is appropriate to decades (if not centuries) of history on top of being wearable day to day (if you can manage the sleeves). 

My 1890s walking skirt. This simple piece stars the wonderful bell-shaped silhouette of the 1890s and the practical bound hem of the Victorian era. The only hand stitching is the backside of the hem binding and the pockets.

**My 1910s wrap cape**
I have a love hate relationship with this work. Given that it’s one of the first things I’ve sewn, and my first historic piece, it is riddled with mistakes. Yet, the over all outcome is pretty close to the goal. It was a good learning project.

### Modern Pieces
Some of the things I’ve made are actually useful! Modern clothes I wear everyday.

The only thing pictured bellow that I didn’t make is the silky tank top. The backless top is a large alteration of a dress. Everything was patterned by heavily adjusting the patterns of my own clothes. 

The corset/stays in this dress began as a separate item (as is pictured in the “Other historical items” section). They were only the 3rd proper thing I sewed and the pattern was drafted through vibes and cardboard with a vaguely 18th century goal. They definitely have issues, but I love them.

### Mending 
Creating something new is exciting, but maintaining something you love is better. When a piece it built to last and the owner shows it love, it can last a lifetime. Each stitch of repair or personalising adjustment adds an emotion value you can’t buy at target.
These fixes don’t take long. They can be hidden or a feature. They are a sign of love. 

But sometimes it’s not about fixing, it’s about customizing. A little tailoring or needed decoration.

This corporate bag, gifted to me at an event hosted by Capital One Cafe, made me sad in capitalism. Rather than toss it away (or donating it, which will likely leave it in landfill anyways), it’s become a project. The logo has been covered, it’s been monogramed, it’s been fished and the next planned addition is a sheep pocket.
